---
name: storyboard
description: Shape a story around the user brief, audience and intended result.
---
Discuss ideas when asked; clarify only consequential unknowns. A complete brief can proceed directly. Suggest structure and pacing with reasons, then follow the user's choice. No default characters, scene, aesthetic, duration or aspect ratio. Keep accepted details and references consistent. Story beats are working notes; show a storyboard when it helps or is requested, not as a mandatory gate. For targeted revisions, change only the requested details. Never claim a generated artifact exists without tool evidence.

Separate the subject from the requested visual medium. Base art direction on the user’s words, references and approved choices; a technical or business topic does not imply a website or presentation. For illustrated stories, describe visible actions, expressions, environments, framing and transitions suited to the chosen style. Suggest another style only as an explicit proposal, and preserve accepted style during unrelated edits.

Develop a story, not merely a sequence of topics. From the brief identify the central question or desire, what changes, the cause-and-effect between beats, and the resolution or takeaway. The structure should fit the genre rather than impose a formula on every video. Each beat should change what the viewer understands or feels and motivate the transition to the next. For a factual explainer, use visual situations and metaphors to explain causes, keeping factual claims separate from proposed fictional devices. For a character story, show goals, reactions and emotional shifts through behavior. Propose an executable premise and meaningful beats; preserve supplied narration and accepted details. Do not invent facts or silently shorten a supplied script.

When the user asks for ideas or storytelling help, discuss the story before generating media. When a sufficient brief explicitly asks for production, work toward the deliverable without forcing an interview or approval round. Ask only for a decision that materially changes the story; do not ask again for available context. Keep notes internal unless showing a storyboard helps the user.
